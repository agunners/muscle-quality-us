Acronyms: DXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry; "DEXA" is the older spelling), BMD (bone mineral density, g/cm²), LSC (least significant change), ISCD (International Society for Clinical Densitometry), RMS-SD / RMS-CV (root-mean-square standard deviation / coefficient of variation of replicate scans), sBMD (standardised BMD, mg/cm²), ALM / ASM (appendicular lean mass / appendicular skeletal muscle mass, kg), EWGSOP2 (European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People, second consensus), MDC95 (minimal detectable change at 95% confidence), ICC (intraclass correlation coefficient), RF (rectus femoris), QMT (quadriceps muscle thickness), df (degrees of freedom).

## 1. Why DXA is the analogy

DXA measures areal bone mineral density in g/cm² from how much of two X-ray energies a bone region absorbs, and a DXA report expresses that number as a T-score (standard deviations from a young-adult reference) and a Z-score (from an age- and sex-matched reference), with osteoporosis defined from the T-score. What turned a modest measurement into the clinical endpoint was not the physics but the discipline around it: every facility measures its own precision on its own patients and derives a least significant change, the smallest change that is more than noise. When a scanner is replaced, the facility scans about 30 patients on both machines before any change is reported across the boundary, and reference databases are made portable across manufacturers by an in-vivo standardisation. Baim 2005 states the number that anchors all of it — the LSC at 95% confidence is 2.77 times the precision error — and our own MDC95 uses the identical constant (1.96 × √2 = 2.77, [reproducibility.py:11](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/scripts/preflight_helpers/reproducibility.py#L11)). We measure a different quantity (RF thickness in mm) with a different modality, but the four DXA pillars — standardised acquisition, stated precision, reference population, cross-calibration — are exactly the scaffold our gates R1–R7 are trying to build ([NORTHSTAR.md:198-206](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/docs/NORTHSTAR.md#L198)). The analogy is a checklist we can be audited against, not a slogan.

## 2. The three papers

### 2.1 Precision and the least significant change — Baim et al. 2005 (ISCD position paper)

Full citation: Baim S, Wilson CR, Lewiecki EM, Luckey MM, Downs RW Jr, Lentle BC. Precision assessment and radiation safety for dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry: position paper of the International Society for Clinical Densitometry. J Clin Densitom. 2005;8(4):371-378.

PMID 16311420 · DOI 10.1385/jcd:8:4:371

Fetched: PubMed abstract record https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=16311420&rettype=abstract&retmode=text and the full text, ISCD's posted copy of the paper https://www.iscd.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/RadiationSafetyWhitePaperrevisedWebPosting2005-06-1.pdf

Status: VERIFIED (abstract and full text read).

What it establishes. Precision is "the ability of a DXA system to obtain consistent BMD values upon repeated measurements of the same patient over a short time"; its error is expressed as the RMS-SD in g/cm² (the ISCD-recommended form) or as a CV in percent. The LSC "that can be recognized with 95% confidence is 2.77 x CV": with 1.0% precision a change must reach 2.77%, with 2.0% precision it must reach 5.6%. The precision study needs 30 degrees of freedom — 30 subjects scanned twice or 15 scanned three times, df = (scans per subject − 1) × subjects — because at 30 df "the upper limit for the 95% confidence interval of the precision error is no more than 34% greater than the calculated value"; subjects must be representative of the clinic's population and the technologist should have scanned at least 100 patients first. Published CVs are total hip 0.8–1.69%, PA spine 1.0–1.2%, femoral neck 1.11–2.2%, trochanter 1.16–1.5%. The manufacturer's precision must not be used: in a multicentre trial six of seven sites had PA-spine precision of 0.969–2.101% and femoral-neck 1.475–3.362%, while the seventh averaged 3.535% (0.230–9.537%) at the spine and 4.349% (0.510–14.148%) at the femoral neck. Positioning and analysis, not the scanner, dominate the error; BMD from different manufacturers "may differ by as much as 10-15%", and identical instruments from one manufacturer still differ by about 2% after primary calibration, "too large for monitoring on different DXA systems". The worked examples make the rule concrete: with a facility LSC of 0.027 g/cm² at the spine, a two-year change of 0.042 g/cm² is a response, a change of 0.012 g/cm² is "no change" even though the hip moved the other way by 0.008 g/cm² against an LSC of 0.030, and a fall of 0.038 g/cm² triggered a work-up that found osteomalacia.

What it means for us. Our MDC95 of 2.19 mm for RF full thickness (CV 4.04%, ICC 0.957, n=116 within-session pairs, [NORTHSTAR.md:52](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/docs/NORTHSTAR.md#L52)) is an LSC in DXA's sense with the same 2.77 constant, and 116 pairs is 116 df, nearly four times ISCD's minimum — that part is already stronger than the ISCD floor. Two things are weaker: our estimator is SEM derived from ICC and SD ([reproducibility.py:81-85](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/scripts/preflight_helpers/reproducibility.py#L81)) rather than the RMS-SD of replicate pairs an ISCD-trained auditor expects, and ISCD precision requires the patient to be repositioned between scans and belongs to a facility and operator, whereas our 2.19 mm is same-session, same-operator — a floor, not the number, until the R3 sub-cohort (25 patients, two operators, 24-hour repeat, [NORTHSTAR.md:202](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/docs/NORTHSTAR.md#L202)) exists. Baim's rule that a manufacturer's precision never applies at a site converts into a product feature: each clinic and operator computes its own LSC in the app, the way ISCD hands out a free calculator. The worked examples are our §4 detectability table ([NORTHSTAR.md:218-223](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/docs/NORTHSTAR.md#L218)) told as clinical stories, and they need what R7 says we lack — a subject key that links two scans of one person ([NORTHSTAR.md:206](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/docs/NORTHSTAR.md#L206)).

### 2.2 Cross-calibration between scanners — Genant et al. 1994 (International DXA Standardization Committee)

Full citation: Genant HK, Grampp S, Glüer CC, Faulkner KG, Jergas M, Engelke K, Hagiwara S, Van Kuijk C. Universal standardization for dual x-ray absorptiometry: patient and phantom cross-calibration results. J Bone Miner Res. 1994;9(10):1503-1514.

PMID 7817795 · DOI 10.1002/jbmr.5650091002

Fetched: PubMed abstract record https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=7817795&rettype=abstract&retmode=xml

Status: VERIFIED (abstract read verbatim; full text not opened).

What it establishes. One hundred women aged 20–80 years (mean 52.6 ± 16; BMD 0.4–1.6 g/cm²) had PA lumbar-spine scans on three scanners from three manufacturers (Norland XR26 Mark II, Lunar DPX-L, Hologic QDR 2000, all pencil-beam), plus the European Spine Phantom and each manufacturer's phantom. Patient correlations between scanner pairs were "excellent", yet the average absolute difference in L2–L4 BMD was 0.012 g/cm² (1.3%) Hologic–Norland, 0.113 g/cm² (11.7%) Hologic–Lunar and 0.118 g/cm² (12.2%) Norland–Lunar. The phantom regression lines only "approximated" the patient lines; the standardisation was built from the intercept and slope of the patient regressions, with the phantom's middle vertebra as a reference point, and expressed as sBMD in mg/cm². After standardisation the average absolute patient differences fell to 28 mg/cm² (2.7%), 23 mg/cm² (2.2%) and 29 mg/cm² (2.8%) for the three pairs; mean BMD went from 0.972 / 1.100 / 0.969 g/cm² (Hologic / Lunar / Norland) to sBMD of 1045 / 1047 / 1043 mg/cm². A patient-based regression, not a phantom, is what "provided compatibility of DXA results obtained on different scanners".

What it means for us. Our 11 same-person pairs across the old and new device+protocol are a Genant study at n=11: full thickness +2.26 mm in 10 of 11 (paired t p=.014), QMT +9.69 mm in 8 of 8 (memory `project_old_new_same_person_pairs`, 2026-08-17), with the code arm measured at 0.00 mm and pressure, site, device and true tissue change surviving (memory `project_bridge_l1_pairs_decomposition`, 2026-08-31) — an offset of the Hologic-versus-Lunar class. Genant's design says how it is closed: a regression with slope and intercept on the same people rather than a mean offset, a residual after bridging (his 2.2–2.8%) that is then added to the LSC for any across-boundary comparison, and enough subjects — he used 100, ISCD's operational minimum is 30 (section 5.1), we have 11. It also names the problem hiding in our placement: the 282-study reference cohort ([per_study_cohort.csv](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/data/cohort_stats/per_study_cohort.csv)) is old-device, so placing a new-device scan against it is the "Lunar patient against a Hologic database" situation that sBMD was invented for; the pre-registered bridge HANDOFF already lists as owed ([HANDOFF.md:270-271](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/HANDOFF.md#L270)) is that standardisation, and the sidebar's "geometry is comparable" copy is ahead of it (memory 08-31).

### 2.3 DXA as the reference for muscle, and its blind spots — Buckinx et al. 2018 (ESCEO working group)

Full citation: Buckinx F, Landi F, Cesari M, Fielding RA, Visser M, Engelke K, Maggi S, Dennison E, Al-Daghri NM, Allepaerts S, Bauer J, Bautmans I, Brandi ML, Bruyère O, Cederholm T, Cerreta F, Cherubini A, Cooper C, Cruz-Jentoft A, McCloskey E, Dawson-Hughes B, Kaufman JM, Laslop A, Petermans J, Reginster JY, Rizzoli R, Robinson S, Rolland Y, Rueda R, Vellas B, Kanis JA. Pitfalls in the measurement of muscle mass: a need for a reference standard. J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle. 2018;9(2):269-278.

PMID 29349935 · PMCID PMC5879987 · DOI 10.1002/jcsm.12268

Fetched: PubMed abstract record https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=29349935&rettype=abstract&retmode=text and full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5879987/

Status: VERIFIED (abstract and full text read).

What it establishes. A European consensus group reviewed every muscle-mass technique and concluded that DXA "could be considered as a reference standard (but not a gold standard)" for muscle lean body mass, on "feasibility, accuracy, safety, and low cost". DXA appendicular lean soft tissue correlates with MRI at r = 0.88 and with CT at r = 0.77–0.95; in-vivo precision for appendicular lean soft tissue is "below 1–3.0%", 4% for bilateral arm mass, and 1.2% CV for total lean body mass (their citation of Hangartner). The limitations are stated plainly: "abnormalities in hydration status (e.g. water retention, heart, kidney, or liver failure) can affect muscle mass measure"; DXA "cannot quantify fatty infiltration of muscle"; lean mass "includes muscle and other components such as skin, tendons, and connective tissues"; it is projectional; and "several devices and several software packages and software versions" give different results. On device transfer: "No phantom has been identified to remove systematic difference in body composition when comparing in vivo results across manufacturers", so "an in vivo cross-calibration study is necessary", and "consensus is required in adopting a reference population in much the same way as has been achieved for the use of DXA in osteoporosis". On ultrasound it records a test–retest ICC of 0.97 (95% CI 0.92–0.99) at the rectus femoris (their cited study, not opened by me), that "a major problem is the impact of the applied pressure on the probe on the measurement result", and that the method "is not widely used for sarcopenia screening and staging" but "may become a valid method".

What it means for us. The incumbent for muscle has our two open problems — device inconsistency with no phantom fix, and no agreed reference population — so the honest analogy is to bone DXA's discipline, not to muscle DXA's current state, which the field itself rates "reference, not gold". The named blind spot "cannot quantify fatty infiltration" is the tissue-quality axis that carries 43% of our condition score, and it is our differentiator only once it is metrologically earned: today `int_cv_within` has MDC÷SD of 1.07 ([NORTHSTAR.md:55](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/docs/NORTHSTAR.md#L55)), its error exceeding the spread between people, and it is mostly brightness (memory `project_int_cv_is_mostly_brightness`). The paper's "major problem" is probe pressure — the surviving arm in our bridge decomposition and the variable the planned L3 returnee session varies on purpose; the literature agrees with our diagnosis. Its closing idea, that a reference standard "with a defined normal range provides a platform on which … new methodologies can be compared", is the route by which ultrasound gets credentialed here: a concordance study against DXA is a validity credential, not a cross-calibration, and the two must not be confused.

## 4. How to press forward

### Technical

- Restate precision in ISCD form: RMS-SD of the replicate pairs in mm, with the degrees of freedom (116) and the 95% CI on the precision error, reported next to the ICC-derived MDC95 of 2.19 mm — same constant, two estimators, nothing for an auditor to ask.
- Run the precision study ISCD would recognise, which is R3: patient re-positioned (off the couch, probe re-placed), two operators, a second day, 25 patients ([NORTHSTAR.md:202](/home/akadir/dev/muscle_mania/docs/NORTHSTAR.md#L202)). Until then every public sentence says "within-session, single-operator". I expect the number to grow, and I would rather we publish the growth than have it found.
- Treat the old→new migration as a Genant/ISCD cross-calibration and nothing less: at least 30 people on both device+protocol within a bounded window (ISCD: 60 days), regression with slope and intercept, the residual reported and added to the LSC for any across-boundary comparison, pre-registered before the numbers travel. Implement ISCD's "do not apply an LSC between instruments that are not cross-calibrated" as a code rule that refuses longitudinal comparison across the boundary, and fix the sidebar copy now.
- Make the reference population device-consistent before it makes more claims: re-acquire it on the new device+protocol, or bridge the 282 with the regression above and carry the bridge residual into the percentile's error bar. Keep calling it placement — EWGSOP2's cut-offs are −2 SD of healthy young adults, and our cohort is neither young nor healthy by construction, so it cannot become a T-score without a different sample.
- Ship DXA's quality-assurance habits as product: a per-site, per-operator LSC calculator (Baim: manufacturer precision never applies at a site), an operator minimum-precision gate with retraining (ISCD's lumbar spine 1.9%, total hip 1.8%, femoral neck 2.5% are the model), the subject key R7 lacks, and the refusal gate R5 calibrated — a gate whose withholds were released 17 times out of 18 on their only audited outing (PPV about 6%, HANDOFF 08-17) is not yet a QA feature.

### Business and positioning

- My recommendation: do not advertise "DEXA-like" as a headline, in any wording, until R3 is closed and the cross-calibration has n ≥ 30. Use the analogy freely in technical and investor conversations as a checklist — "we are building the four DXA disciplines for muscle ultrasound: stated precision, LSC, reference population, cross-calibration" — because it is true, auditable, and the vocabulary pharma and core-lab buyers already own.
- The risk of the phrase is threefold. It invites the wrong comparison: DXA is a whole-body, ionising, non-portable mass measurement, so a buyer hears "replaces DXA" and a regulator hears an equivalence claim we cannot support. It caps the story at DXA's ceiling, which for muscle is only "reference, not gold" (Buckinx 2018), and hides the one thing DXA cannot do — see tissue quality — which is our upside if we earn it. And a diligence reader will test each of the four pillars; today we hold one of four (within-session precision), so the phrase becomes an overclaim on first contact.
- The words to own instead: "muscle thickness with a stated least significant change of X mm" and "placement against a device-consistent reference range, or a refusal". A number with an error bar is the DXA lesson; the brand name is not.
- Position against DXA as its bedside complement, not its replacement: EWGSOP2 advises DXA to confirm sarcopenia and records ultrasound as "reliable and valid" with "good validity … as compared to DXA, MRI and CT" (Cruz-Jentoft 2019). The credential this community understands is a concordance study against DXA on the same people; plan it as a validity study, keep it separate from the device bridge, and align landmarks and settings with SARCUS (Perkisas 2021) so the external standard we cite is the one geriatricians already use.
- Sequence: the DXA analogy does nothing for the kill-shots (IP/FTO against Mayo US10722219, consent/ethics, clinical utility, SWE foreclosure — memory `project_strategy_stress_test_2026jun`); those stay first. Within the analogy, the cheapest credibility is precision-as-product (the per-site LSC tool) sold in core-lab dialect to pharma/CRO lean-mass endpoint buyers (HANDOFF 08-17 persona audit) — the same buyer who already pays for DXA's LSC.

## 5. Two supporting references

### 5.1 Shepherd et al. 2006 — the 2005 ISCD Official Positions on cross-calibration and minimum precision

Full citation: Shepherd JA, Lu Y, Wilson K, Fuerst T, Genant H, Hangartner TN, Wilson C, Hans D, Leib ES; International Society for Clinical Densitometry Committee on Standards of Bone Measurement. Cross-calibration and minimum precision standards for dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry: the 2005 ISCD Official Positions. J Clin Densitom. 2006;9(1):31-36. PMID 16731429 · DOI 10.1016/j.jocd.2006.05.005.

Fetched: PubMed abstract record https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=16731429&rettype=abstract&retmode=text (VERIFIED, abstract only) and the current ISCD Official Positions page https://iscd.org/learn/official-positions/adult-positions/ (fetched; current revision, not necessarily the 2005 text).

What it establishes. The abstract lists the four positions adopted in 2005: cross-calibration when changing or replacing hardware; the approach when an entire system is changed to one by the same or a different manufacturer; what to do when no cross-calibration or BMD comparison is done between facilities; and the minimum acceptable precision for an individual technologist. The current ISCD page carries the operational numbers: "Scan 30 patients representative of the facility's patient population once on the initial system and then twice on the new system within 60 days"; for an added scanner of the same model, one spine phantom on both scanners on 20 different days, with more than 0.5% difference in mean BMD sent back to the manufacturer; "Do not apply an LSC or report BMD change between instruments that are not cross-calibrated"; "Patients should return to the same DXA device that was used to perform their most recent prior study"; precision by "15 patients 3 times, or 30 patients 2 times, repositioning the patient after each scan"; minimum acceptable precision of lumbar spine 1.9% (LSC 5.3%), total hip 1.8% (LSC 5.0%), femoral neck 2.5% (LSC 6.9%); and "Retraining is required if a technologist's precision is worse than these values".

What it means for us. This is the migration protocol to copy line by line (section 4, technical bullet 3), and the operator rule — a numeric minimum with retraining — is what our operator self-check lacks.

### 5.2 Cruz-Jentoft et al. 2019 — EWGSOP2, the muscle quantity/quality distinction

Full citation: Cruz-Jentoft AJ, Bahat G, Bauer J, Boirie Y, Bruyère O, Cederholm T, Cooper C, Landi F, Rolland Y, Sayer AA, Schneider SM, Sieber CC, Topinkova E, Vandewoude M, Visser M, Zamboni M; Writing Group for EWGSOP2 and the Extended Group for EWGSOP2. Sarcopenia: revised European consensus on definition and diagnosis. Age Ageing. 2019;48(1):16-31. PMID 30312372 · PMCID PMC6322506 · DOI 10.1093/ageing/afy169. Erratum: Age Ageing 2019;48(4):601.

Fetched: PubMed abstract record https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=30312372&rettype=abstract&retmode=text and full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6322506/ (VERIFIED, full text read).

What it establishes. Sarcopenia is "a muscle disease (muscle failure)"; low strength is the key characteristic, low muscle quantity or quality confirms it, and poor performance marks severity (the Find-Assess-Confirm-Severity pathway). Cut-offs: grip strength <27 kg men / <16 kg women; ASM <20 kg / <15 kg; ASM/height² <7.0 / <5.5 kg/m²; gait speed ≤0.8 m/s. On DXA: "different DXA instrument brands do not give consistent results" and measurements "can also be influenced by the hydration status of the patient", yet "DXA is advised in clinical practice" to confirm. Muscle quality refers to "micro- and macroscopic changes in muscle architecture and composition, and to muscle function delivered per unit of muscle mass", with "no universal consensus on assessment methods for routine clinical practice". On ultrasound: "reliable and valid and is starting to be used at the bedside"; the EuGMS consensus protocol covers thickness, cross-sectional area, fascicle length, pennation angle and echogenicity; "echogenicity reflects muscle quality, since non-contractile tissue associated with myosteatosis shows hyper-echogenicity"; ultrasound "was shown to have good validity to estimate muscle mass as compared to DXA, MRI and CT". Normative cut-offs are set at −2 SD of healthy young adults (−2.5 SD for a conservative diagnosis), and "some cut-off points are arbitrary at this time".

What it means for us. The community already applies a T-score-shaped rule to muscle, but against young healthy adults, which our cohort is not — so our percentiles stay placement. "Echogenicity reflects muscle quality" is the field's language for our texture axis, and it is device- and gain-dependent; we should not borrow the sentence until the gain sweep and a repeatability floor make the axis ours.

Also opened this session but not summarised, to keep this doc short: Bonnick et al. 2001, J Clin Densitom 4(2):105-110, PMID 11477303 (the older statement of the same rule: until precision studies are performed "the least significant change cannot be determined … making the interpretation of serial studies impossible"); Hui et al. 1997, J Bone Miner Res 12(9):1463-1470, PMID 9286763 (re-derives the sBMD conversions without residual bias; researchers should "standardize their own machines in a laboratory"); Jankowski et al. 2019, J Clin Densitom 22(4):472-483, PMID 31558404 (the 2019 ISCD position on LSC across multiple scanners); Perkisas et al. 2021, Eur Geriatr Med 12(1):45-59, PMID 33387359 (SARCUS 2020 update: standardised landmarks for 39 muscles, nine ultrasound parameters including echo-intensity).
